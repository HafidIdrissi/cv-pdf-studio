# Writing playbook

Read this before drafting a summary, achievement, or cover letter. Write for a recruiter who
needs to see role fit and evidence quickly, without sounding generated or formulaic.

## Professional summary

Use a summary only when it adds positioning that the experience section cannot show as quickly.
Aim for roughly 40–70 words and adapt voice to the language and market; first-person writing is
optional, not mandatory.

Build it from three elements:

1. Truthful professional identity or clearly labelled target role.
2. One or two high-value capabilities supported by master IDs.
3. A relevant outcome, scope, or domain signal.

Avoid generic claims such as passion, dynamism, rigor, or motivation unless evidence makes them
specific. Do not open with a degree unless the qualification itself is decisive for the role.

## Achievement bullets

Prefer: **strong verb + specific contribution + outcome or scope + relevant method or tool**.

- Give the most relevant recent role the most evidence.
- Keep most bullets to one or two typeset lines.
- Vary verbs and remove task-only openings such as `Responsible for`, `Participation in`, or
  `In charge of` when a concrete action exists.
- Use a number only when the matching achievement record supports it.
- Use precise qualitative evidence when measurement is unavailable: shipped artifact, users
  served, risk removed, decision owned, stakeholder group, or process changed.
- Keep keywords in meaningful context rather than stacking them in a list.

## Selection and length

Target relevance without erasing career continuity. Sort retained entries by date, never by
relevance.

- Prefer one page for early- and mid-career profiles when it remains readable.
- Allow two pages for roughly 10+ years, leadership, complex technical scope, research,
  publications, or several roles that directly prove fit.
- Put decisive evidence and the recent timeline on page one.
- Include projects when they add evidence absent from employment; reduce them for experienced
  candidates whose work history already proves the requirement.
- Do not enforce a fixed maximum number of roles or projects independently of page strategy.

## Cover letter

Write a letter only when requested or materially useful. Aim for approximately 180–300 words,
one page, and four compact moves:

1. **Specific hook** — connect a real company or role detail to candidate evidence.
2. **Proof** — unpack one relevant problem, contribution, outcome, and technology overlap.
3. **Contribution** — state what the candidate could credibly contribute in the first months.
4. **Close** — give availability when known and invite a conversation.

Do not paraphrase the CV. Mirror the employer's register. Address a named person when supplied.
Bridge an adjacent skill honestly; never disguise a gap as experience.
